The Data on Data Privacy

Data has been described as the "currency of the 21st century." As more of our personal information becomes digitised, the need for our data to be respected and protected has become increasingly critical. Throughout this visualisation, examine the legal protections in place to protect data privacy in Australia and in Europe, consider enforcement measures taken for privacy breaches and explore how the Australian public's view on data privacy has evolved over time.

Australia: Legislative History and Protections

Federal Legislation

The most critical privacy legislation in Australia is passed at the Federal level, led by the Privacy Act 1988. The Privacy Act outlines which information is protected, penalties for breaches and importantly sets out the Australian Privacy Principles which govern how applicable business must handle personal information. Recent legislative amendments have increased penalties and introduced the statutory tort of serious breach of privacy, providing persons a new avenue to use to courts to pursue their rights. The National Health Act, Telecommunications Act and Criminal Code Act also contain some provisions related to data privacy within their respective fields.

State Legislation

The Australian states have taken varied approaches to passing their own privacy legislation. New South Wales were the first state to pass data privacy focused law, with the Privacy Committee Act 1975 predated the federal Privacy Act. Given the existence of the federal Privacy Act, state legislation seeks only to fill gaps that are specifically related to that particular state. For instance, the Victorian Privacy and Data Protection Act 2014 is limited to only cover the Victorian public sector. Hence, the late adoption of legislation in Western Australia and the absence in South Australia does not mean the people from these states were left without applicable data privacy legislation. Note: Not all legislation with reference to privacy is included, only dealing with major privacy enhancing law.
